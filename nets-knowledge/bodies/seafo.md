## Mandate and area

SEAFO is a regional fisheries management organisation (RFMO) established under the
Convention on the Conservation and Management of Fishery Resources in the South East
Atlantic Ocean. Its area is the **high seas** of the South East Atlantic, outside national
jurisdiction. Its **Scientific Committee** advises the Commission on stocks and on
ecosystem questions, including protection of **vulnerable marine ecosystems (VMEs)** from
bottom fishing.

## What it typically needs from a survey

- Information from **seamounts and other high-seas features**: species composition,
  deep-water fish (e.g. orange roughy, alfonsino, toothfish, deep-sea red crab), size
  structure, and relative abundance where estimable.
- **VME indicator taxa** encountered, and habitat information relevant to closed areas and
  bottom-fishing footprints.
- Bycatch and biodiversity observations; environmental context.
- Methods in enough detail to judge what the observations can and cannot show (gear,
  depth range, effort, identification level).

## Synthesis profile

- **Purpose:** link to the Scientific Committee agenda item (e.g. VME review, stock status).
- **What the survey found:** by feature or area block, at a spatial resolution agreed with
  the user; VME indicator encounters as presence/extent at agreed resolution.
- **Limitations:** sampling effort per feature, gear selectivity, identification level of
  benthic taxa, depth coverage.
- **Implications:** for VME management and for stock information where data are scarce.

## Sensitivity notes

- **Positions.** VME locations are shared with RFMOs to support protection, but precise
  positions of aggregations of commercial deep-water species can attract targeting. Agree
  the spatial resolution with the user (and data owner) before drafting. NETS masks
  sub-degree positions by default.
- High-seas results: record who clears them (`not_required` with a scope note, or the
  programme's decision-maker).
- Check the body's rules on confidentiality of data and documents.
